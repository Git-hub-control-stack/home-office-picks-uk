# Best Monitor Light Bar UK 2026

Put an ordinary lamp on a desk in front of a screen and a good share of its light comes straight back off the glass at you. A monitor light bar answers that by sitting on the top edge of the screen and throwing its light forward, onto the desk rather than the display. What separates a good one from a bright plastic strip is not lumens. It is how faithfully the light shows colour, whether the white can be moved from warm to cool, and whether the bar sets its own brightness or waits for you to do it. This guide leads with those three, and with which of them each maker will actually put a figure to, because that is where this category gets slippery.

## The three things that matter more than lumens

1. **Colour rendering (Ra or CRI).** Two names for one measure: how faithfully a light shows colour against daylight, scored up to 100. Our own bar for desk work is 90 or above, because under that, skin tones and printed colour start to look a little off even in a room that feels bright enough. Xiaomi quotes Ra95 and Quntis quotes Ra95 or higher. BenQ quotes nothing we could verify, which on the premium pick is the most irritating gap in this guide.
2. **Colour temperature.** One fixed white suits nobody all day: cooler light suits the morning, warmer light suits the hours when you have had enough of it. A maker that quotes a real span, as Xiaomi does with 2700K to 6500K, is telling you the bar moves between the two. A maker that quotes nothing is telling you to find out for yourself once it arrives.
3. **Auto-dimming, or your own hand.** An auto-dimming bar changes its brightness as the room changes; the alternative is that you do. Neither is wrong, and the sensor buys convenience rather than better light or truer colour. Our own scoring puts a figure on that: the budget pick has auto-dimming, the overall pick does not, and the two finish within a tenth of a point of each other.

One thing no spec sheet spells out: these bars work because of the shape of the lens, which pushes light down and forward onto the desk while cutting what spills back towards the screen. That is the mechanism behind a phrase like BenQ's Glare-Free, and it is why a light bar and a desk lamp are not the same purchase. ## FAQ

**Q: Does a monitor light bar actually reduce glare on the screen?**
A: That is what the shape is for. The light is aimed down at the desk instead of out at the screen, so much less of it bounces off the glass back at you. It is an angle fix rather than a medical one, and it will do nothing about a bright window sitting behind your monitor.

**Q: What do Ra and CRI mean, and why does the number matter?**
A: They are two names for the same measure: how accurately a light shows colour compared with daylight, scored up to 100. We look for 90 or above for desk work. Below that, colours under the light can look washed out even in a room that feels perfectly bright.

**Q: Do I actually need auto-dimming?**
A: Most people do not. It saves you setting the brightness yourself; it does not make the light better or the colour truer. If your desk sits in fairly steady light, setting a bar once and leaving it alone is no hardship.

**Q: Will a light bar fit my monitor?**
A: They sit on the top edge of the screen, so fit comes down to your monitor's top edge and, on a curved screen, how far the curve pulls the ends of a straight bar away from the panel. Only one of our three publishes its own length, and none of them publishes a bezel or curvature figure we could verify, so the bar's own listing is where to check before ordering.

**Q: Can I use a light bar with a monitor arm?**
A: Yes, and little changes: the bar sits on the monitor rather than on the desk, so it travels with the screen whenever you move the [arm](best-monitor-arm-uk.html). Get the arm's fit right first, since it is the thing carrying both.
