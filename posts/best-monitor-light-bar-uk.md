# Best Monitor Light Bar UK 2026

A monitor light bar clips above the screen and lights the desk in front of you rather than the screen itself, which is the whole design brief: an ordinary desk lamp throws light back off the glass and straight into your eyes. Three numbers separate a good bar from a bright plastic strip, and none of them is lumens. The colour rendering index (Ra or CRI) decides whether things under the light look like their real colour instead of flat and grey. The colour-temperature range decides whether you get one fixed white or a genuine span from warm to cool. ## The three numbers that matter more than lumens

1. **Colour rendering (Ra or CRI).** This is how faithfully a light reproduces colour compared with daylight, on a scale that tops out near 100. Look for 90 or higher: below that, skin tones and printed colour under the light start to look slightly wrong, even if the room feels bright enough. The budget pick states Ra95 or higher and the overall pick states Ra95; the premium pick's listing gives no figure at all.
2. **Colour-temperature range.** A single fixed white suits nobody all day: mornings often want cooler light to wake a room up, evenings want warmer light that is easier on tired eyes. A bar that states a genuine range, like the overall pick's 2700K-6500K, lets you move between the two. A bar with no published range is probably fixed.
3. **Auto-dimming versus a dial.** An auto-dimming bar reads the room with a sensor and adjusts itself; a dial bar leaves the adjustment to you. Neither is wrong, and most people do not strictly need the sensor - a good manual bar at a third of the price does the same core job of lighting the desk instead of the screen. The sensor buys convenience, not more light.

A proper light bar's lens is also shaped on purpose: it throws light down and forward onto the desk while cutting the spill back toward the screen, which is the actual mechanism behind "reduces glare on the screen" rather than a marketing line. ## FAQ

**Q: Does a monitor light bar actually reduce glare on the screen?**
A: That is the design intent behind the asymmetric lens shape these bars use: light is aimed down at the desk, not out at the screen, so less of it bounces back off the glass into your eyes. It is a lighting-angle fix, not a medical one.

**Q: What do Ra and CRI mean, and why does the number matter?**
A: Both describe how accurately a light source reproduces colour compared with daylight, on a scale that tops out near 100. A figure of 90 or above is generally considered strong for desk work; below that, colours under the light can look slightly washed out even in a bright room.

**Q: Do I actually need auto-dimming?**
A: Most people do not. It buys convenience, not more light or better colour rendering: a good manual bar with a wireless dial does the same core job of lighting the desk instead of the screen, for less money.

**Q: Will a light bar fit any monitor?**
A: Most bars in this category clip onto the top edge of a flat monitor without tools. A very thin bezel or a curved screen can make the clip a tighter fit, so check the bar's own listing for clearance before you buy, especially if your monitor is unusual.

**Q: Can I run a light bar on a monitor arm instead of a desk stand?**
A: These are screen-top bars, not standalone lamps, so they mount to the monitor itself rather than the arm or the desk. If the monitor is on an [arm](best-monitor-arm-uk.html), the bar moves with it, which is one more reason to get the arm's fit right first.
