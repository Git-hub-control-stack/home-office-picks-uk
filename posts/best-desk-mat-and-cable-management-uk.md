## Wire it in this order

Most desk-tidying goes wrong because the cables get routed before anyone decides where the power lives. Work outwards from the block instead.

1. The block goes in the box first. Everything downstream is measured from where it ends up, so settle that before a single cable is routed. Block in, its own lead out to the wall, lid closed.
2. Route by zone rather than by cable. Do the screen first, then anything the desk itself powers, such as a sit-stand motor, then the things you unplug most weeks. Each zone travels to the box as its own bundle, so one bundle can be pulled later without disturbing the others. If the screen hangs on a [monitor arm](best-monitor-arm-uk.html), send its cable down the pole before it joins anything.
3. Leave slack wherever the desk moves. On a sit-stand desk, measure every run at full height, never sitting down. The lead that reaches comfortably at desk height is the one that unplugs itself the first time somebody raises the desk to standing.
4. The mat goes down last. It's the one item you'll want to lift and reposition, and it shouldn't be pinning a bundle you've just dressed.

## When a tray beats a box

A box lives where the block lives: on the floor, at the back of the desktop, inside a cupboard. A tray fixes underneath the desktop, screwed or clamped depending on the model, and carries the block with the desk, which is what a sit-stand owner really wants, since a floor-standing box turns every height change into a tug of war with the lead. ## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a desk mat and a cable box, or will one do?**
A: They fix different halves of the same complaint. The mat changes what you look at and rest on; the box changes what you see under and behind the desk. If you only want one, pick the half that annoys you when you sit down.

**Q: What size desk mat should I get?**
A: Measure your keyboard plus the space your mouse actually travels, then check your desk is deeper than the mat before you order. Width is the easy half to get right; the back edge is where orders go wrong, especially once a monitor foot or an arm clamp is sitting there.

**Q: How do I hide cables on a desk without drilling?**
A: A box for the block, adhesive clips along the underside of the desktop for the runs, and a sleeve for the drop to the floor. That covers most desks with nothing screwed in. Trays and channels are the step up, and those usually do want fixing to the desk.

**Q: Is it safe to put an extension block inside a box?**
A: Treat the block's own instructions as the authority, not us: stay inside its rated load, keep high-draw appliances like heaters off it, and don't shut a lid on a tightly coiled lead. D-Line describes this box as made from electrically safe ABS, which is the maker's claim about its own product. We haven't tested it, so we're not going to tell you that enclosing a block makes no difference.
